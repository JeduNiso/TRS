# 05 - UX Guidelines

Project:
TRS+ Bolivia Corporate Website

Version:
1.0

Status:
Approved

---

# 1. UX Philosophy

The website must not simply present information.

It must build trust.

Every interaction should reduce uncertainty.

Every screen should reinforce professionalism.

The user should never wonder:

"Can this company handle my business?"

The interface should answer that question before it is asked.

---

# 2. Primary UX Goal

The primary goal of the website is not page views.

The primary goal is confidence.

Every interaction should increase the user's confidence in TRS+.

The website should feel like speaking with an experienced corporate consultant.

Not with a salesperson.

---

# 3. User Mental Journey

The experience should guide visitors through five psychological stages.

Stage 1

Attention

"This company looks professional."

↓

Stage 2

Trust

"They appear organized and reliable."

↓

Stage 3

Authority

"They clearly know what they are doing."

↓

Stage 4

Value

"They can solve our operational problems."

↓

Stage 5

Action

"I should contact them."

Every page should support this progression.

---

# 4. First Five Seconds

Within the first five seconds the visitor should immediately understand:

Who TRS+ is.

What TRS+ does.

Why TRS+ is different.

How to contact TRS+.

No scrolling should be required to answer those questions.

---

# 5. Navigation Principles

Navigation should be effortless.

Users should never wonder where to click.

Maximum top-level navigation items:

6

Avoid dropdown menus unless absolutely necessary.

Current page should always be visually highlighted.

Navigation must remain visible while scrolling.

---

# 6. Reading Behavior

Users scan.

They do not read.

Design every section for scanning.

Use:

Headings

Subheadings

Bullet lists

Icons

Cards

Whitespace

Avoid large paragraphs.

Maximum paragraph length:

4 lines.

---

# 7. Information Hierarchy

Every section should answer one question only.

Example

Who are we?

↓

What do we offer?

↓

Why trust us?

↓

How do we work?

↓

How do I contact you?

Never mix unrelated topics inside one section.

---

# 8. Cognitive Load

Reduce cognitive effort.

Do not overwhelm visitors.

Rules:

One primary action per section.

Maximum two buttons.

Maximum one featured image.

No visual clutter.

No unnecessary animations.

---

# 9. Visual Rhythm

Alternate layouts to maintain attention.

Example

Text Left

Image Right

↓

Image Left

Text Right

↓

Cards

↓

Full Width CTA

Avoid repeating identical layouts throughout the page.

---

# 10. Trust Building

Every scroll should increase credibility.

Examples

Professional photography

Operational processes

Technology

Coverage

Security

Corporate language

Real business benefits

Never use exaggerated marketing language.

---

# 11. Call To Action Strategy

Every CTA should answer:

"What should I do next?"

Examples

Request a Consultation

Schedule a Meeting

Talk With Our Specialists

Explore Our Services

Avoid

Submit

Read More

Click Here

---

# 12. Hero Experience

The Hero has one responsibility.

Generate confidence.

The Hero must include:

Headline

Supporting text

Primary CTA

Secondary CTA

Professional image

Trust indicators

Nothing more.

---

# 13. Scrolling Experience

Scrolling should feel natural.

Each section should introduce new information.

Avoid repetitive layouts.

Avoid empty sections.

Avoid unnecessary scrolling.

---

# 14. Mobile Experience

Mobile is not a smaller desktop.

It is a different experience.

Rules

Large touch targets.

Readable typography.

Stacked layouts.

Sticky CTA.

Simple navigation.

Fast loading.

No horizontal scrolling.

---

# 15. Forms

Corporate forms should feel simple.

Minimum required fields.

Clear labels.

Immediate validation.

Visible success feedback.

Avoid long forms.

---

# 16. Images

Every image should communicate something.

Avoid decorative stock photography.

Preferred subjects

Operations

Technology

Vehicles

Monitoring

Corporate meetings

Professional teams

Infrastructure

Avoid generic smiling office workers.

---

# 17. Motion Design

Motion should support usability.

Never distract.

Allowed

Fade

Reveal

Slide

Floating

Scale

Hover

Not Allowed

Bounce

Shake

Blink

Continuous spinning

Aggressive parallax

---

# 18. Empty Space

Whitespace is intentional.

Do not fill empty areas unnecessarily.

Whitespace improves readability and perceived quality.

---

# 19. Error Prevention

Users should never become lost.

Every page should include:

Visible navigation.

Clear CTA.

Logical content flow.

Readable typography.

Consistent spacing.

---

# 20. Accessibility

Keyboard navigation.

Visible focus.

WCAG AA contrast.

Semantic HTML.

Screen reader friendly.

Every interaction must remain usable without a mouse.

---

# 21. Performance UX

Perceived speed is as important as actual speed.

Use:

Skeleton loaders (future)

Image lazy loading

Smooth transitions

Avoid layout shifts

Prioritize above-the-fold content

---

# 22. Enterprise UX

Remember the audience.

Decision makers.

CEOs.

Operations Managers.

Financial Managers.

Security Managers.

They value:

Clarity

Efficiency

Professionalism

Evidence

Reliability

Not entertainment.

---

# 23. Emotional Goal

The user should finish browsing with this thought:

"TRS+ understands operational complexity."

Not

"This website looks nice."

---

# 24. Success Criteria

The UX is successful when users can answer these questions without confusion:

Who is TRS+?

What problems do they solve?

Why should I trust them?

What should I do next?

If any answer is unclear,

the UX has failed.

---

# 25. Golden Rule

Every interaction must reduce uncertainty.

Every scroll must increase confidence.

Every page must move the visitor one step closer to becoming a client.
